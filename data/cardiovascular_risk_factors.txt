Cardiovascular Disease: Risk Factors and Prevention

Overview
Cardiovascular disease (CVD) encompasses conditions affecting the heart and blood vessels,
including coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmias, and stroke. It remains one of
the leading causes of morbidity and mortality among adults.

Modifiable Risk Factors
- Hypertension: sustained high blood pressure damages arterial walls over time and is one of
  the strongest modifiable risk factors for CVD.
- Dyslipidemia: elevated LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, combined with low HDL cholesterol,
  contribute to atherosclerotic plaque formation.
- Tobacco use: smoking damages blood vessel linings, raises blood pressure, and promotes clot
  formation.
- Diabetes and insulin resistance: chronic hyperglycemia accelerates vascular damage and
  atherosclerosis.
- Obesity and physical inactivity: excess body weight and sedentary behavior are associated
  with hypertension, dyslipidemia, and insulin resistance.
- Unhealthy diet: diets high in saturated fat, sodium, and added sugar increase cardiovascular
  risk, while diets rich in vegetables, fruits, and whole grains are protective.
- Excessive alcohol consumption and chronic stress also contribute to elevated risk.

Non-Modifiable Risk Factors
Age, sex, family history of early cardiovascular disease, and genetic predisposition all
influence baseline cardiovascular risk and cannot be changed through lifestyle intervention.

Prevention Strategies
Primary prevention focuses on lifestyle modification: regular aerobic exercise (at least 150
minutes per week), a heart-healthy diet such as the Mediterranean or DASH diet, maintaining a
healthy body weight, smoking cessation, moderating alcohol intake, and managing stress.
Routine screening for blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood glucose helps identify at-risk
individuals early. For patients with elevated risk, statin therapy, antihypertensive
medications, and antiplatelet therapy may be recommended in addition to lifestyle changes,
based on individualized risk assessment.

Warning Signs
Symptoms that warrant urgent evaluation include chest pain or pressure, shortness of breath,
pain radiating to the arm, jaw, or back, sudden weakness or numbness (especially on one side
of the body), slurred speech, and sudden severe headache.
