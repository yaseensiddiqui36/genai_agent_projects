Hypertension (High Blood Pressure): Clinical Overview

Definition
Hypertension is defined as a sustained systolic blood pressure of 130 mmHg or higher, or a
diastolic blood pressure of 80 mmHg or higher, measured on two or more separate occasions.
Blood pressure is categorized as normal (below 120/80 mmHg), elevated (120-129/<80 mmHg),
stage 1 hypertension (130-139/80-89 mmHg), or stage 2 hypertension (140/90 mmHg or higher).

Risk Factors
Common risk factors for hypertension include advancing age, family history, obesity, high
sodium intake, low potassium intake, physical inactivity, excessive alcohol consumption,
tobacco use, chronic stress, and comorbid conditions such as diabetes and chronic kidney
disease.

Complications
Untreated or poorly controlled hypertension increases the risk of stroke, myocardial
infarction, heart failure, aortic aneurysm, peripheral artery disease, chronic kidney disease,
and vision loss due to hypertensive retinopathy.

Management and Blood Pressure Targets
First-line management includes lifestyle modification: reducing dietary sodium to less than
2,300 mg per day, adopting the DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) eating pattern,
achieving and maintaining a healthy body weight, engaging in at least 150 minutes of
moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, limiting alcohol intake, and smoking cessation.

For most adults with confirmed hypertension, the recommended blood pressure target is below
130/80 mmHg. Pharmacologic treatment options include thiazide diuretics, ACE inhibitors,
angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), and calcium channel blockers, often used in combination
when a single agent is insufficient. Regular home blood pressure monitoring is recommended to
track treatment response.

Follow-Up
Patients starting or adjusting antihypertensive therapy should be reassessed within one month
to evaluate response and tolerability. Once blood pressure is at goal, follow-up every three
to six months is typically appropriate, along with periodic monitoring of kidney function and
electrolytes for patients on diuretics or renin-angiotensin system inhibitors.
