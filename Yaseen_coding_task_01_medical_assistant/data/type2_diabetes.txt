Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus: Management Guide

Definition and Diagnosis
Type 2 diabetes is a chronic metabolic condition characterized by insulin resistance and
relative insulin deficiency, resulting in elevated blood glucose levels. Diagnostic criteria
include a fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher, a hemoglobin A1c of 6.5% or higher,
a random plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher with classic symptoms, or an abnormal oral
glucose tolerance test.

Lifestyle Management
Lifestyle modification is the foundation of Type 2 diabetes management. Recommended changes
include:
- Adopting a balanced diet emphasizing vegetables, whole grains, lean protein, and healthy
  fats while limiting refined sugars and processed carbohydrates.
- Engaging in at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise, combined
  with resistance training two to three times per week.
- Achieving a 5-10% reduction in body weight for patients who are overweight or obese, which
  has been shown to meaningfully improve glycemic control.
- Smoking cessation and limiting alcohol consumption.
- Regular self-monitoring of blood glucose as advised by a care team.

Pharmacologic Therapy
Metformin is typically the first-line pharmacologic agent unless contraindicated. Additional
agents such as SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, or
insulin may be added based on individual glycemic targets, comorbidities, and cardiovascular
or renal risk.

Glycemic Targets
A general hemoglobin A1c target for most non-pregnant adults is below 7%, though targets may
be individualized (e.g., relaxed to below 8% for older adults with comorbidities, or
tightened for younger, healthier patients without hypoglycemia risk).

Complications and Monitoring
Chronic hyperglycemia can lead to microvascular complications (retinopathy, nephropathy,
neuropathy) and macrovascular complications (coronary artery disease, stroke, peripheral
artery disease). Recommended monitoring includes annual dilated eye exams, annual urine
albumin-to-creatinine ratio testing, foot exams at every visit, and A1c testing every three to
six months depending on how well glucose is controlled.
